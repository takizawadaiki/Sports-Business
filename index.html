Metric definitions & formulas

Gini Coefficient
G = 1 − (2/n) × Σ(i=1→n) [ (n−i+0.5) × s_i / Σs ]
Measures salary inequality within a team roster. 0 = perfect equality, 1 = maximum inequality. Computed live from the player salary CSV layer using actual 2025-26 contract data.
Payroll Efficiency ($/Win)
Efficiency = Team Active Payroll ($M) / Regular Season Wins
How many millions spent per regular-season win. Compare within leagues only — NBA/NHL play 82 games, NFL plays 17. Dead cap is excluded from payroll figures where possible.
Labor Share of Revenue
Labor Share = Total League Payroll / Est. League Revenue × 100
Each CBA defines its base differently: NBA uses BRI (Basketball Related Income, ~$10.25B in 2024-25), NHL uses HRR (Hockey Related Revenue, ~$7.7B), NFL uses AR (All Revenue, ~$23B).
Max Player % of Cap
Max% = Top Single-Player Salary / League Salary Cap × 100
NBA 2025-26: Curry $59.6M / $154.6M = 38.6%. NHL: Draisaitl $14M / $95.5M = 14.7%. NFL: Prescott $60M AAV / $279.2M = 21.5%.
Cap Per Roster Spot
Cap/Spot = League Salary Cap / Active Roster Size
NBA (15 spots): $154.6M / 15 = $10.31M. NHL (23 active): $95.5M / 23 = $4.15M. NFL (53 active): $279.2M / 53 = $5.27M.
Revenue Per Active Player
Rev/Player = League Revenue / (Teams × Active Roster)
NBA 30×15=450 players: $14.3B/450 = $31.8M. NHL 32×23=736: $7.7B/736 = $10.5M. NFL 32×53=1,696: $23B/1,696 = $13.6M. Roster size is the key driver of this metric, not league quality.
Cap Growth Index
Index_t = Cap_t / Cap_2015 × 100 (2015 baseline = 100)
Normalizes all three leagues to a 2015 baseline for direct growth comparison. 2025-26 index values: NBA 221, NHL 138, NFL 195. The NBA's 2016 TV deal spike (34% in one year) remains the largest single-season cap jump in major pro sports history.

CBA structure — key terms

NBA apron system (2025-26): Soft cap $154.6M → Luxury tax $187.9M → First apron $195.9M → Second apron $207.8M. Teams over the second apron lose most signing exceptions and cannot make salary-increasing trades. Phoenix Suns paid an estimated $152M in luxury tax in 2024-25 alone.

NHL LTIR: Long-term injured reserve allows teams to temporarily bank space above the ceiling while injured players recover. Cannot spend over cap in playoffs. Tampa Bay and Toronto have historically used this mechanism.

NFL dead cap: Guaranteed money owed to released/traded players still counts against the cap. Average NFL team carries $15-25M dead cap annually. 2025 NFL cap $279.2M is the first time it cleared $275M; 2026 cap is set at $301.2M — the first time it will exceed $300M.

NHL cap trajectory: $95.5M (2025-26) → $104M (2026-27) → $113.5M (2027-28). Kirill Kaprizov's $17M/yr contract beginning 2026-27 will be the largest AAV in NHL history.

Executive Summary · 2025-26

Six findings that define
professional sports economics

Original analytical conclusions drawn from verified 2025-26 salary cap data, revenue figures, and competitive balance metrics across the NBA, NHL, and NFL.

Finding 01 · NBA
Steph Curry is earning 38.6% of the salary cap — the highest single-player cap share in league history
$59.6M / 38.6%
At $59,606,817, Curry is the highest-paid player in the NBA for 2025-26 — and his share of the $154.647M cap is the highest ever recorded. With the cap rising 10% (the CBA-maximum) this season, supermax contracts are now hitting numbers that would have seemed unthinkable five years ago. For context, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander — the reigning NBA champion, MVP, Finals MVP, and scoring champion — earns just $38.3M, because his historic supermax extension doesn't kick in until 2027-28. OKC got a significant bargain during their championship run.
Finding 02 · NHL
The NHL cap finally broke free — up $7.5M in one year, with $18.5M more coming by 2027-28
$95.5M → $113.5M
After years of flat or barely-moving caps following COVID, the NHL announced a three-year roadmap: $95.5M (2025-26) → $104M (2026-27) → $113.5M (2027-28). The league posted a record $7B+ in revenue for 2024-25. This trajectory is transforming the player market: 20 players now earn $10M+, up from 17 last season. Leon Draisaitl's $14M AAV is currently the league's highest — but that record will fall when Kirill Kaprizov's $17M/yr deal kicks in next season, the richest in NHL history.
Finding 03 · NFL
The NFL salary cap surpassed $300M for the first time — and quarterback economics now dominate everything
$279.2M → $301.2M
The 2025 NFL cap hit $279.2M, a $23.8M jump from 2024. The 2026 cap is set at $301.2M — the first time in league history it has cleared $300M. QBs dominate the earnings landscape: Dak Prescott leads at $60M AAV, followed by four players at $55M (Allen, Burrow, Lawrence, Love). The top 16 highest-paid NFL players are all quarterbacks. This concentration raises a structural question: as QB contracts consume 20%+ of the cap, does roster depth inevitably suffer?
Finding 04 · NBA
OKC Thunder won the championship with a below-market payroll — the most efficient title run in recent NBA history
68-14 · ~$155M
The Thunder's 2024-25 championship season came with one of the lowest payrolls among contenders. SGA's $38.3M is well below what comparably-elite NBA stars earn because he's still on his prior contract. Chet Holmgren and Jalen Williams are also on rookie-scale deals. This is the "rookie contract window" efficiency premium that teams like OKC, built through the draft, are designed to exploit. The window closes in 2027-28 when SGA's supermax (~$60M+/yr) kicks in — making the next 2 seasons a crucial competitive window for the Thunder.
Finding 05 · NHL
Florida Panthers won back-to-back Stanley Cups — the hard cap's immunity to dynasties is overstated
2× Cups · 2024 & 2025
The Panthers won consecutive Stanley Cups in 2024 and 2025, challenging the narrative that the NHL's hard cap prevents dynasties. Their approach — signing core players like Barkov, Tkachuk, and Reinhart to team-friendly long-term deals before market corrections — shows that cap management quality matters more than cap level. The Panthers spent efficiently, stayed cap-compliant, and retained their core through smart extensions, not financial dominance. This is the NHL model of dynasty: roster construction intelligence, not payroll.
Finding 06 · Cross-league
Revenue per active player is diverging rapidly — NBA players now generate $31.8M each vs $10.5M in the NHL
3.0× gap NBA vs NHL
With projected NBA revenue of $14.3B across 450 active players, each NBA player "generates" ~$31.8M in league revenue. NHL players generate ~$10.5M each ($7.7B / 736 players). NFL players generate ~$13.6M each despite the NFL's $23B revenue, because 1,696 active players split the pie. The NBA's new $76B media deal — worth double its prior contract — is widening this gap further, setting up the NBA's 2026+ BRI calculations to push cap projections toward $166M and beyond.
Finding 07 · MLB — No Cap
The Dodgers' luxury tax bill alone exceeds the entire payroll of 12 MLB teams — the cap-free model creates the widest spending gap in pro sports
4.0× spread · $342M vs $87M
MLB is the only major North American pro sports league with no salary cap. The 2025 CBT (luxury tax) threshold is $241M — but it's a soft deterrent, not a hard ceiling. The LA Dodgers paid a $169M luxury tax bill in 2025, more than the entire payroll of the Miami Marlins ($87M) or Chicago White Sox ($92M). The top-to-bottom payroll ratio of ~4.0× (Mets $342M vs Marlins $87M) dwarfs any capped league. In the NBA, NFL, and NHL that ratio is under 2.0×. Juan Soto's $765M deal — the richest in pro sports history — and Shohei Ohtani's $700M contract both exist because MLB allows truly uncapped spending by large-market teams.
